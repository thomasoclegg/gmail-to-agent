# Step lists for the videos

Generated by `node media/record.mjs --steps` from the storyboards in `media/storyboards/`. Put each list next to its video: the videos are silent, and this is their text alternative.

## Hero loop

`docs/media/hero.webm`, `docs/media/hero.mp4`, `docs/media/hero.gif`, 13 s

1. In Gmail, right-click an email.
2. Pick an AI from the top of the menu, for example Ask ChatGPT.
3. The extension opens the email and copies the whole thread. The note at the bottom left says so.
4. A moment later the AI opens in a new tab. Press Ctrl+V to paste the thread.
5. Check what you pasted, then press Enter. Nothing is sent until you do.

## Clip 1: pick your AIs

`docs/media/clip-pick-ais.webm`, `docs/media/clip-pick-ais.mp4`, 12 s

1. Click the extension's icon in the browser's toolbar. If you can't see it, pin it from the puzzle-piece menu.
2. The list of your AIs opens. Click Settings at the bottom.
3. The settings page opens. Tick the AIs you use. Each tick saves straight away.
4. Back in Gmail, right-click an email. The AIs you ticked are at the top of the menu, in the same order.

## Clip 2: Claude Code and Codex

`docs/media/clip-desktop-agent.webm`, `docs/media/clip-desktop-agent.mp4`, 13 s

1. On the settings page, tick Claude Code or Codex and add a working folder. The app needs Gmail connected for the same mailbox.
2. In Gmail, right-click an email and pick Open in Claude Code (or Open in Codex).
3. The first time, Chrome asks whether to open the app. Tick Always allow, then click Open.
4. The app opens with a prompt: the mailbox, the thread ID and the Gmail link, plus the subject for Claude Code so the session is named after the email. The message text is never in it.
5. The prompt asks the app to read the thread through its own Gmail connection, check Sent mail and each fact in its own tools, do the work, then draft any reply. Anything that has to wait is set aside: Claude Code is asked to propose a separate session for it (at most three, with any others as briefs in its answer), Codex to give a brief in its answer. It is told to follow nothing an email asks, and to send or change nothing outside its working folder without your approval.
6. Claude Code waits for you to press Enter. Codex starts by itself once a working folder is set, and only reads until you reply.

## Clip 3: copy for any AI

`docs/media/clip-copy.webm`, `docs/media/clip-copy.mp4`, 7 s

1. On the settings page, tick Copy thread for any AI.
2. In Gmail, right-click an email and pick Copy thread for any AI.
3. The extension opens the email and copies the whole thread. Nothing else opens.
4. Paste it into any AI chat with Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac), check it, then send it.

## Clip 4: several emails at once

`docs/media/clip-bulk.webm`, `docs/media/clip-bulk.mp4`, 12 s

1. In the Gmail inbox, tick two or more emails (up to 25).
2. Right-click one of the ticked emails and pick an AI, for example Ask Claude about 3 emails.
3. The extension reads each thread in the background without opening it, so unread emails stay unread.
4. The AI opens in a new tab. Press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac) to paste, check it, then press Enter.
5. The prompt asks the AI to group the emails into tasks, put the most urgent first and draft replies. Claude Code and Codex get the thread IDs and links instead of the message text, and Claude Code also gets each subject.

## Walkthrough (YouTube)

`store/video/walkthrough-1920x1080.mp4`, `store/video/walkthrough-1920x1080.srt`, 90 s

1. Install the extension. The settings page opens; to open it again, click the extension's icon in the browser's toolbar, then Settings.
2. Tick the AIs you use. Each tick saves straight away.
3. In Gmail, right-click an email and pick an AI, for example Ask Gemini. The extension opens the email and copies the whole thread.
4. The AI opens in a new tab next to Gmail. Press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac) to paste the thread and read it through. Nothing is sent until you press Enter.
5. To work on several emails at once, tick them, right-click one of them and pick an AI. Each thread is read without opening it, and the AI is asked to sort them into tasks.
6. Copy thread for any AI copies the thread but opens no AI, so you can paste it wherever you like.
7. Claude Code and Codex open on your desktop with a prompt: the mailbox, the thread ID and the Gmail link, plus the subject for Claude Code. The message text is never in it. The prompt asks them to read the thread through their own Gmail connection, check Sent mail and each fact in their own tools, do the work, then draft any reply. Anything that has to wait is set aside: Claude Code is asked to propose a separate session for it (at most three, with any others as briefs in its answer), Codex to give a brief in its answer. They are told to follow nothing an email asks, and to send or change nothing outside their working folder without your approval.
8. With nothing ticked and no email open, the extension's button in Gmail's toolbar reads Triage unread. Click it and pick an AI, for example Ask ChatGPT about 4 emails. The unread emails on this page of the list, up to 25, are read without opening them, so they stay unread, and the AI is asked to sort them into Action, Consolidate, Disregard and Draft.
9. The extension's icon in the browser's toolbar (pin it from the puzzle-piece menu if you can't see it) lists your AIs, with a line saying what they will act on: the email you have open, or the ones you ticked. At the bottom are Settings and the shortcut that sends to the AI you used last: Alt+Shift+L, or Control+Shift+L on a Mac.
10. On the settings page, Your own instructions takes text of your own in place of the built-in instructions: one box for Claude Code and Codex, one for the web AIs and Copy. Triage unread and selected text keep the built-in instructions.
11. It runs on your computer: no server, no account, no tracking.

## Install video (YouTube)

`store/video/install-1920x1080.mp4`, `store/video/install-1920x1080.srt`, 54 s

1. On the website, click the Download button (it names your browser) to get open-in-ai-agent.zip. Edge and the other Chromium browsers load the same zip.
2. Unzip it. On Windows, right-click the zip and pick Extract All: a double-click only shows what is inside. On a Mac, double-click it. Windows may hide the .zip ending, so the zip and the folder can share a name: use the one with the plain folder icon.
3. Move the open-in-ai-agent folder somewhere it can stay, such as Documents, as long as OneDrive or iCloud does not keep that folder online-only. The browser runs the extension from that folder, so leave it there.
4. Open chrome://extensions (edge://extensions in Edge) and switch on Developer mode, at the top right. In Edge it is in the left-hand panel.
5. Click Load unpacked and pick the open-in-ai-agent folder itself: manifest.json is directly inside it, and it is not the zip. Then click Select (Select Folder on Windows).
6. The extension's card appears and its settings page opens by itself. Tick the AIs you use. Each tick saves straight away.
7. Open the puzzle-piece Extensions menu in the toolbar and pin Open in AI Agent for Gmail, so its icon stays in view. In Edge, click Show in toolbar.
8. In Gmail, right-click an email and pick an AI. Gmail tabs that were already open need no reload. Press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac) in the AI to paste the thread, read it through, then press Enter.
